1. Patient Registration & Demographics ModuleThis module captures the core identification and contact details required to register a patient in the system.Patient Identification: Name and Hospital Registration Number.  Personal Details: Age, Sex (Male/Female), and Marital Status (Married, Single, Divorced).  Background: Tribe/Nation and Occupation.  Contact Information: Telephone number.  Physical Address: P.O. Box, Street, House Number, Area/Ward, and Town/Municipality/City.  2. Clinical History & Anamnesis ModuleThis module tracks the patient's medical background, reasons for visiting, and specialized historical data.Chief Complaint: The primary reason for the visit, populated during registration.  General Medical History: Checkboxes or fields for Diabetes, Blood Pressure, Asthma, Allergies, Heart conditions, Major surgeries, HIV/AIDS, and Pregnancy (noting 1st, 2nd, or 3rd trimester).  Current Medication: Type and dosage of any drugs currently in use.  Family, Social, and Developmental History: General family/social history, along with prenatal and postnatal history.  Past Dental History: Records of previous attendance and reasons (e.g., check-up, extraction, scaling, restoration, prosthesis).  Presenting Dental History (Pain Assessment): Tracking the onset (spontaneous vs. non-spontaneous), severity (mild, moderate, severe), character (localized vs. not localized), quality (dull, burning, sharp, throbbing), duration, radiation, and aggravating/relieving factors.  Reported Symptoms: Cavities, swelling, pus discharge/fistula, halitosis, bleeding gums, loose dentures, or ulceration.  3. Clinical Examination ModuleThis module organizes the physical assessment into general, extra-oral, and intra-oral categories.Vital Signs & General Assessment: Blood Pressure, Pulse, Height, Weight, Posture, Gait, and a general appearance indicator (Healthy-looking vs. Ill-looking).  Extra-Oral Examination:Facial metrics including head shape, facial form, symmetry, proportions, and profile.  Skeletal relationships (anterior, posterior, and vertical).  Aesthetic markers such as smile line, smile angle (corridor in mm), nasolabial angle, chin, and mentolabial sulcus.  Lip competence (competent vs. incompetent).  Temporomandibular Joint (TMJ) assessment evaluating tenderness, sounds, jaw deviation, and restricted movement.  Lymph node palpability.  Intra-Oral Examination:Soft tissue health covering the tongue, palate, gingiva/mucosa, and periodontium.  Hard tissue and dentition status, noting un-erupted, missing, decayed, filled, defective, worn, or discolored teeth.  Presence of deposits (plaque and calculus) mapped by sextant.  Occlusion status (normal vs. malocclusion) and presence/status of prostheses.  Record of oral habits.  4. Investigations, Imaging & Models ModuleThis section handles the storage and evaluation of diagnostic media and tests.Photography: Extra-oral and intra-oral facial photographs mapped with dates.  Physical/Digital Models: Pictures of study models and clinical evaluation notes of the models.  Radiography: Periapical x-ray reports, radiolucent/radio-opaque area detection, and root/furcation analysis.  Pulp Testing: Results for percussion, cold tests, heat tests, and test cavities.  5. Diagnosis & Treatment Planning ModuleThis module bridges the examination findings with actionable clinical steps.Diagnoses: A ranked problem list, working diagnoses, and final clinical impressions.  General Treatment Plan: A comprehensive, sequenced dental and medical action plan.  Consultations: Routing for internal/external referrals (Medical/Surgical, Oral Surgery, Orthodontics/Pedodontics, Operative, Periodontics, Prosthodontics).  6. Specialized Operative Care ModuleThis module logs detailed procedural steps, specific materials, and grading criteria for restorative and endodontic work.Direct & Indirect Restorations:Tracking of cavity sizes (small, medium, large) and classes (I-V).  Material selection options (Amalgam, Gold, Composite, Glass Ionomer, Ceramics).  Quality scoring rubrics for functional properties (marginal adaptation, contacts, wear), biological properties (postoperative sensitivity, pulp status), color match, and finishing.  Endodontic Therapy:Procedure type (Pulpotomy, Pulpectomy, Root Canal Treatment).  Anatomical data including tooth length, number of canals, and working length in millimeters.  Technique details, such as preparation method (conventional, step-back, step-down), irrigating agents, dressing materials (Eugenol, Cresophane, CPCP), and obturation records across multiple visits.
